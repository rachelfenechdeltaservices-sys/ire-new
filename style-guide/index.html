Style Guide

This guide documents the finished homepage of the US sweepstakes casino so designers and developers can rebuild it and extend it to new pages. It covers colour, type, borders, buttons, image style and every homepage section, top to bottom.

The source of truth is the latest full homepage (with Jackpots and the 96%+ RTP panel). Three close-ups add detail: High RTP Games (game cards only), New Games and Favourite Characters. Where a close-up and the homepage disagree, the homepage wins. Anything no longer on the page has been removed.

How to read the values. Sampled means the colour was read from a pixel in the screenshot. Matched means it was matched by eye, because glows, gradients and fonts can't be recovered exactly from a flat image. Sizes are measured from the screenshot and scaled to a 1440px canvas, so treat them as ±4px.

Two families, both free on Google Fonts. Inter sets the Headings: clean, even capitals at a regular weight. Imagery

Image style

All game and promo art follows one look: glossy, high-contrast 3D illustration lit by neon. Every image uses the same recipe. Each point below can be seen in the approved examples.

Always

  • One clear subject: a character, a dealer, or a single symbol (book, 7, clover, diamond).
  • Neon rim light in the card's accent colour. Light trails or sparks curl around the subject.
  • Deep background: navy, black-violet, or one saturated hue for New Games.
  • Rich, saturated colour with crisp highlights on gold, gems and coins.
  • Friendly, confident characters looking at the viewer, from the chest up.
  • Game logo in the lower third (tiles) or upper right (character cards).

Never

  • Photographs, flat vector art or muted colour.
  • Buttons, prices, coin amounts, RTP numbers or player counts inside the image.
  • A border or glow drawn into the image. Borders are always CSS.
  • Imitations of a real provider's game art. Licensed titles use the provider's own approved assets.
  • Cash, cards or chips shown as real-money prizes. This is a sweepstakes site (see wording).
  • Stretching. Crop to the ratio instead.
